\begin{abstract}
We describe a hypothesis-neutral software experiment that tests whether pulsar
single-pulse feature streams contain repeated, protocol-like modulation not
reproduced by fitted natural and statistical models. The pipeline is fully
preregistered: feature channels, detector, surrogate ensembles, negative
controls, escalation thresholds and a negative-result policy were frozen before
any real data were examined, and the single preprocessing amendment made during
the campaign was registered with an A/B verification before use. Across seven
frozen experiments on public Breakthrough Listen data --- 45 observations of
four pulsars and two blank-sky fields from two independent observatories (GBT
and Parkes), totalling 63{,}005 pulse folds and ${\sim}400$ registered analyses
--- zero candidates survive the registered detection ladder. All four Level-1
statistical anomalies that arose were attributed to instrumental gain wander by
the control battery, including two blank-sky detections with compression gains
an order of magnitude above anything seen on-source. We report three
methodological findings of standalone value for single-pulse technosignature
searches: (i) integer-sample folding silently destroys pulse alignment for
rotation periods that are non-integer multiples of the sampling interval;
(ii) other beams of a multibeam receiver are not valid off-source controls for
bright pulsars due to sidelobe detection; and (iii) a canonical instrument
signature --- uniform Level-1 firing across all negative controls with
split-half incompatibility --- cleanly separates backend artifacts from any
credible sky-borne candidate. The full pipeline, frozen manifests, SHA-256
input provenance and per-observation reports are public and reproducible.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and scientific boundary}

Searches for engineered structure in astrophysical time series face an
asymmetric risk profile: false positives are cheap to generate and expensive to
retract. This work does \emph{not} assume pulsars host artificial
transmitters. It operationalizes a narrower, falsifiable question:

\begin{quote}
Can observed per-rotation pulse features be explained by fitted
natural/statistical models, or do they contain repeated, self-consistent
structure that warrants independent follow-up?
\end{quote}

A positive software result is an anomaly, never a detection claim. The
registered escalation ladder requires, above the software level: raw-data and
instrument-team review, off-source exclusion, predicted recurrence in a
held-out epoch, and independent-observatory confirmation before the phrase
``technosignature candidate'' applies.

\section{Pipeline design}

\subsection{Feature extraction}
Dynamic spectra (SIGPROC filterbank, Breakthrough Listen HDF5, or search-mode
PSRFITS) are incoherently dedispersed, RFI-masked per channel (robust
median/MAD statistics), optionally cleaned of zero-DM broadband bursts, and
folded at the topocentric rotation period into one feature vector per rotation
across four registered channels: on-window amplitude, a signed polarization
proxy, an intensity-weighted arrival-time residual, and a frequency centroid.

Folding is performed in \emph{fractional phase}: sample $i$ is assigned phase
$\phi_i = (i / P_s) \bmod 1$ with $P_s$ the (generally non-integer) period in
samples. A registered two-stage period refinement maximizes folded-profile
sharpness only, and is never applied to deliberately mis-folded controls.

\subsection{Detector}
Each channel is binarized by robust two-cluster thresholding; frame candidates
are found by normalized autocorrelation over lags 32--4096; significance is
assessed against a mixed surrogate ensemble (first-order Markov, run-length
permutation, circular block bootstrap) with a four-channel look-elsewhere
correction; zlib compression gain relative to the surrogate median acts as a
protocol-likeness metric. Surrogates are deterministic per index
(\texttt{SeedSequence(seed, spawn\_key=(index,))}), so $p$-values are
independent of parallel scheduling --- verified bit-identical between serial
and 16-process execution.

\subsection{Controls and dispositions}
Every observation is accompanied by: a block-permutation control (preserving
local texture), three off-pulse windows, two wrong-period folds, two wrong-DM
reductions, and a split-half recurrence check; batches receive
Benjamini--Hochberg FDR correction. A target reaching the registered Level-1
thresholds (channel-adjusted $p<0.05$, compression gain $>128$ bits) is
\emph{rejected} if any control is at least as anomalous, if it fails split-half
compatibility, or if it fails batch FDR ($q<0.01$).

\section{Validation}

\textbf{Injected positives.} A seeded red-team benchmark of 24 cases (12
natural, 12 with injected repeated frames) yields precision $=$ recall $=1.0$
at the operating point (12/12 true positives, 0/12 false positives).
Injection-recovery on real extracted trains recovers $1\sigma$ and $2\sigma$
injections at 2/2 each with 0/2 zero-strength false alarms.

\textbf{Real-world true negatives.} Two blank-sky star-field scans from the
same GBT session and backend as the pulsar data, folded at the Crab ephemeris
with a NULL expectation registered beforehand, produced the loudest raw
anomalies of the campaign (frequency-channel compression gains of 3{,}408 and
2{,}424 bits at the surrogate-resolution $p$ floor) --- and both were rejected
automatically by uniform control firing with incompatible split halves.

The v0.5 survey identified a lag-${\sim}36$ frequency-centroid periodicity in
the Crab data (336-bit gain, split-half replicated) that fired identically in
all seven dynamic controls and in an unrelated pulsar --- fast per-channel
gain wander of the GBT/BL backend. Amendment 1 (a moving-median per-channel
detrend across rotations, window ${\sim}33$ rotations, registered with an A/B
verification: gain $336 \to 0$, adjusted $p\,0.0078 \to 1.0$) removed it; the
v0.6 re-run then exposed a slower wander (lag ${\sim}472$, ${\sim}16$\,s)
which the off-pulse controls rejected live, and the v0.9 blank-sky runs proved
both components exist with no source in the beam.

In the 25-epoch Vela batch the minimum adjusted $p$ was $0.055$ ---
consistent with the expected minimum among 25 independent draws under the
null --- and the 25 per-epoch best lags show no cross-epoch agreement.

\section{Methodological findings}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fractional-phase folding is mandatory.} At 349.5\,$\mu$s
sampling, PSR B0329+54's period is 2044.26 samples; integer-sample folding
drifts 0.26 samples/rotation and reduced the folded-profile peak $z$ from 99.4
(fractional) to 0.0009 (integer) --- a bright pulsar rendered invisible by a
common implementation shortcut.
\item \textbf{Multibeam ``off'' beams are not off-source for bright pulsars.}
Vela is detected in every beam of the Parkes multibeam receiver via sidelobes
(fold $z$ up to 158 in beams pointed away). Valid off-source controls require
separate blank-sky pointings.
\item \textbf{A canonical instrument signature.} In all four instrument-caused
Level-1 events: uniform Level-1 firing across every negative control, and
split-half incompatibility. A credible sky-borne candidate must exhibit the
inverse (target $\gg$ controls; split-half compatible), then survive the
held-out epoch and an independent observatory.
\item \textbf{Amendment discipline is practical.} One preprocessing amendment
sufficed for the campaign; registering it with a pre-verified A/B on the
identified artifact, applied identically to targets and controls, preserved
the audit trail without freezing the pipeline into known errors.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope of the null and reproducibility}

The null constrains repeated-frame modulation in four per-rotation feature
channels at 32--4096-rotation periodicities, rotation periods 0.03--0.7\,s,
1.0--3.4\,GHz, minute-scale scans, at the sensitivity established by
injection-recovery --- for the specific epochs analyzed. It makes no claim
beyond that scope.

Every experiment is a frozen YAML manifest with SHA-256-pinned inputs;
runs are deterministic given the seed; outputs include timestamped logs,
per-observation checkpoints and machine-readable dispositions. The software
(Python, ${\sim}$3{,}000 lines, 20 automated tests), all frozen manifests,
experiment reports and input provenance records are public at
\url{https://github.com/dragoscv/notalone} (release v1.0.0, MIT license).
